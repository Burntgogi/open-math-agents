# Independent review of the one-percent bound

I find the written proof valid. It gives a zero limit for the coverable proportion at the budget in Green39.green_39.variant_101. I checked the November 28, 2026 workspace copy of `one-percent.md`, SHA-256 `650d267c953f4a92168dcad0a17535c1f1e20a90653cbfc99f43f07a02ced4a7`, against upstream revision `9097e2f90e66d2f6244209cb1c07f7120d17ec53`. The probabilistic proof is on paper. The Lean files in this directory cover parts of its arithmetic and the final logical implication.

## The fixed-container test

Write Ω for the uniform slice of all k-subsets, d for the number of replacements between two members of Ω, and m for the cardinality of a deterministic container P. Avoiding the m-point set y−P has probability ρ = (p−m)ₖ/(p)ₖ, using falling factorials. For p sufficiently large, m ≤ Mk gives m/(p−j) ≤ 1/2 and k m/(p−k+1) ≤ 2M. Thus ρ ≥ exp(−4M), uniformly even for very uneven containers. A single exchange uncovers at most m sites and covers at most m sites. The absolute change in the number of holes is at most m. Exposing an ordered sample, transposing the two proposed next elements pairs their conditional completions at distance at most one. Azuma therefore gives a failure bound of 2 exp(−δ²p²ρ²/(2km²)) for the lower hole count when m>0. This exponent has order at least k for fixed M and δ.

For t outside P, condition on one label a being selected. The other k−1 labels must avoid a+t−P, wholly in the remaining population of p−1. Summing over a gives k(p−1−m)ₖ₋₁/(p−1)ₖ₋₁ = kρp/(p−m). Including t in P gives score zero. Using an independently resampled hole set in this calculation would miss the factor p/(p−m).

For L = ⌈k^0.1⌉, the probability that some translate of **+P** contains L sample elements is at most p (eMk²/(pL))ᴸ. Its logarithm is at most −c k^0.1 log k eventually. The desired regular domain allows L hits, so this is also an upper bound on its complement. Between any two regular samples at distance d, a lost contribution either uses one of the d deleted labels or comes from an unchanged label hit by a newly inserted center v. There are at most L unchanged labels in v−t+P for each v. Reversing the samples bounds the absolute score difference by (L+1)d. This direct comparison avoids any need for an exchange path that stays regular.

The minimum of the distance cones extends the score from this nonempty domain with the same Lipschitz bound. Clipping to [0,k] preserves it. The extension's mean differs by at most k times the irregularity probability. Uniformly in m, p/(p−m) tends to one and ρ is bounded below. The upper threshold (1+δ)kρ consequently leaves deviation at least δkρ/2 for all sufficiently large p. The score failure costs one irregularity event per P plus 2p exp(−c k^0.8). The three failure exponents beat any fixed power of log p in the log size of the terminal family. For m=0 the counts are exact. A cover must hit every hole, whence the simultaneous lower bound outside P is (1−δ)p/((1+δ)k). For example δ=0.01 suffices for the coefficient 0.98. Only at this last counting step is T quantified. Arbitrarily many covers are allowed.

## From a cover to fitted witnesses

1. **Split.** At a point of multiplicity d≥2 there are d nonprivate labels and d−1 excess representations. Since d≤2(d−1), there are at most 0.02p nonprivate incidences. At least (1−0.02/0.0225)p/k = p/(9k) centers are good. Choose a single cover as a function of the unordered sample before randomizing its order. Conditional on that sample, the count of a good center's nonprivate labels in half j has mean at most 0.01125k+0.01125. An exchange changes it by at most one. For large k an upper deviation of 0.0001k suffices for the tail estimate at 0.0114k. Union over both halves and at most p centers gives O(p exp(−c k)). Summing conditional failures over coverable samples costs this same bound, without dividing by the probability of a cover.
2. **Incidences.** Distinct private pairs from the first half yield distinct values, so |U|≥r(k₁−0.0114k). Every term counted from the second half in a translate by a center of S meets a first-half representation with a different center. Hence the sum of observed overlaps is at most 0.0114ks, even when R intersects S. Since the initial cover has at most 1.01p/k<2k eventually, the entire good set fits the size restriction.
3. **Fitting.** The reference law restarts at the uniform probability on all p sites after *each* deletion and at *each* level. The target law is uniform on the remaining F. Its initial relative entropy is log(p/|F|)≤log p. Both a signed character test and the negative-heavy-set test have target-minus-reference mean at least η/2. Tilting by η/4 and applying the bounded log-moment estimate gives a KL decrease of at least 3η²/32. Each run therefore has at most 16η⁻² log p updates. Removing the heavy set consumes at least η|F|/2 labels, so after at most 2+⌈2η⁻¹log(2/ξ)⌉ deletions fewer than ξk remain. Each recorded heavy set has size at most Kk. At a smooth stop the discarded mass is at most η < 1/2, normalization raises the cap to at most 2/(Kk), and the Fourier error is at most (2+√2/2)η.
4. **Decoding.** A prefix of signed character tests and negative-heavy-set tests determines the current law, its heavy set, and the law obtained by trimming. Each symbol has at most 4p+1 choices. Include the empty prefix, and enumerate only admissible trimmed laws for the smooth list. A law or single heavy set costs O(η⁻² log² p) in log cardinality. A container costs this times the maximum number of deletions, plus the encoding of the length. This gives the uniform upper exponents 0.44+o(1) and 0.66+o(1), respectively, since p/k is asymptotic to k. Only the container at the immediately preceding level is used to count R,S. For the final test the last active level has 5000<x≤10000, so its **own** container family costs O(log² p). Charging all earlier transcripts to this last test would not justify its union bound. The last container is decoded from just its own list of heavy sets. The shrinkage inequality has slack 341.03776 at x=5000, and a uniform surplus greater than 300 is proved in Lean.
5. **Fourier subsampling.** For s=|F| there is nothing to sample. In the remaining case s=⌊0.001k⌋. On that uniform slice of F, changing one label changes a coordinate sum by at most two for a real or imaginary character. A union over 2p coordinates costs O(p exp(−c sη²)). The least sη² is at least k^(0.56−o(1)), so a suitable S exists for each F. This is a deterministic existential conclusion before the witness union.

## Smooth laws under the ordered split

For each half of size n, an individual convolution coordinate has mean n/p and variance ≤(n/p)Σμ². This follows by expanding the variance of the inclusion indicators with nonpositive distinct-site covariance and nonnegative weights. Jensen and summation give expected ℓ¹ error ≤√(pnΣμ²) ≤2k/√K eventually. This last comparison uses p<(k+1)², n≤(k+1)/2 and Σμ²≤2/(Kk). The bound 2·10⁻¹² is strictly below ε=10⁻⁸ after division by k. Exchanging a sample label costs at most two in ℓ¹. Each marginal failure therefore costs 2 exp(−c ε²k), for either orientation of the kernel. These tails beat the log size k^(0.44+o(1)) of the smooth list.

For a sample of n weights from N sites bounded by m₀, the normalized degree-n elementary symmetric sum of exp(w/m₀) is its moment generating function. Maclaurin bounds this by the nth power of the mean. The chord bound on [0,1] then gives exp((e−1) E Z/m₀). For a fixed first-half coordinate use m₀=2/(Kk), mean k₁/p and threshold k^(−1/2). Its log tail is at most (e−1)K/4 − (K/2)√k. Conditional on the entire first half satisfying its sup and ℓ¹ bounds, translate the positive excess above 0.51/k. It has total mass at most εk since k₁/p≤0.5/k. The expected sum over the second half is at most εkk₂/(p−k₁) ≤2ε. Reapply the same moment bound on the complement with m₀=k^(−1/2). At threshold 0.001 the exponent is at most −(0.001−2(e−1)ε)√k. Both square-root rates dominate k^(0.44+o(1))+log p. The second-half marginal ℓ¹ bound is intersected with this event. The proof never draws from a second half already conditioned to be typical.

## Excluding a fit

Summing the baseline over A₂ uses the convolution with μ, linearity, and |U|. Bounding the baseline on E uses all representations a+v of each u in U and the reflected law, since μ(a+v−z) occurs in g(z−v). Counting every representation gives an upper bound because the weights are nonnegative. For k≥10,000, r/k≥0.0999, k₁/k≥0.49995, k₂/k≥1/2, and p/k²<(1+1/10000)². The resulting lower baseline coefficient exceeds 0.2442260, and the observed coefficient is at most 0.1141142. Thus on the typical and balanced events a purported fit would give

> 0.244 ≤ 0.115 + 0.001 + 0.001 + 0.51 |E|/k.

Here one 0.001 is the off-E discrepancy and the other is the clipped excess bound. In particular |E|/k ≥ 127/510 > 0.24. Fix the enumerated container P, R,S and capped law, with the claimed Fourier approximation. Given **only A₁**, the bad set of all z is deterministic. The discrepancy is the convolution of 1_U with the reflection of 1_S−sμ. Unnormalized Fourier coefficients and counting ℓ² give mean square ≤(7ηs)² |U|/p. At the threshold 0.001rs/k the bad fraction is ≤(71000η)² because 7/(0.001·0.099)<71000. The denominator during subsequent ordered exposure is ≥p−k, giving a per-slot conditional hit bound q=2(71000η)²<1. For any prescribed set of h slots, iterated conditioning gives probability ≤qʰ. Summing over slot sets of size ⌈0.24k⌉ costs at most 2ᵏ. This probability computation places no good-center, coverability, or balance restriction on the conditional sample.

Take the union over fixed witnesses after obtaining this bound. For N≤keˣ, the standard binomial estimate uses u(1+log(keˣ/u)), increasing up to the relevant caps 0.1k and 0.001k. Deal with size zero separately. Counting all sizes and all levels adds o(k) to log cost, as do the previous-container and smooth-law lists. There is no cost for the choice of T, V or F because a failure with these objects implies a witness in the smaller list. For each x the net log probability per k is at most

> −473.4405223566… − (23/5000)x + o(1).

The o(1) is uniform, using the largest lists over all levels. The two coefficients of x before cancellation are 0.101 and −0.1056. Paying for a full container transcript at *every witness choice* is allowed here because k^0.66=o(k). Charging it later against the fixed-container test would require a different argument.

## Termination and the exact target

On the common event excluding all fits, retain just the centers from the current V lying in the next container. Comparison with the reverse geometric series gives total loss <4k/(3·5000²). Hence more than 0.11k survive throughout. Eventually there is an active level, and the final size is ≤e^5000 k. The constant M=e^5000 is fixed independently of p. Intersect the earlier event on the unordered slice requiring at least 0.98p/k centers outside each terminal candidate. The surviving centers are inside it, so the cover would have size ≥0.98p/k+0.11k>1.01p/k, using p/k≤2k eventually. Therefore the cover-existence event lifted to ordered samples is contained in the union of the explicit failure events. Its probability equals its unordered probability because every k-set has exactly k! orders.

For positive p, k²≤p, hence ⌊1.01k⌋≤⌊101p/(100k)⌋. The rational numerator in the upstream definition counts exactly the corresponding coverable unordered sets and its denominator counts the full slice. Domination by the larger-budget probability tending to zero gives zero limit, incompatible with limit one on the prime subtype. No quantitative bound on the first useful prime follows from the estimates here.

Formalize the exchange and bounded-weight sampling lemmas once for arbitrary finite populations. Build the transcript lists by recursion independent of A, with budget, soundness and cardinality proved separately.
