# Review of the fixed-gap theorem

**Verdict.** I find the proof of the \(1+10^{-26}\) lower bound valid as written. I found no exceptional graph shape or adaptive choice that defeats it. The accompanying [one-percent proof](one-percent.md) derives the stronger bound \(1.01p/k\) by replacing the terminal argument and rebalancing the earlier exposure. Both conclusions are asymptotic. This is a mathematical review and the new proof has not had a separate referee or Lean verification.

The manuscripts examined were the versions of fixed-gap.md, report.ko.md and hubs.ko.md with SHA-256 prefixes F08EA59A23D8, 72DF3C77D401 and 976C73565F08, respectively. The originals have been left as supplied.

## Fixed-gap proof, in dependency order

1. **Volume loss and private labels.** For a cover the loss is exactly \(k|T|-p\). Every point of multiplicity \(m\ge2\) contributes \(m\le2(m-1)\) nonprivate incidences. Therefore the good-center count is at least \((1-2c/b)p/k=0.98p/k\ge0.98k\), and the stated \(0.97k\) bound has slack. The same argument includes centers concentrated on one point of very high multiplicity. Private first-half incidences are distinct, and a second-half label witnessing contact with \(A_1+R\) must use a different center from its first-half representation. Both inequalities (2) are deterministic for arbitrary \(R,S\), including \(R=S\) or partial overlap.

2. **A typical event for a deterministic list.** A single convolution coordinate is a sum of nonnegative population weights on the uniform slice. Its variance is bounded by \((k_j/p)\sum\mu^2\). Summing square roots gives the stated \(2k/\sqrt K\) expectation bound. The exchange difference of the norm is at most 2 since a probability law has mass one; a transposition couples whole completions of an ordered sample. The positive gap \(\varepsilon-2/\sqrt K\) gives an exponential tail in \(k\). The symmetric-mean estimate for capped weights is valid without replacement. It gives the two additional tail exponents \(k^{1/3}\) and \(k^{2/3}\); both dominate \(k^{1/5}+\log p\). The conditional excess expectation divides by \(p-k_1\), so \(2\varepsilon\) is valid. The \(A_2\) marginal concentration and the conditional excess estimate are intersected. Neither is used as conditioning information for the subsequent fixed-witness probability.

3. **Stopping and descriptions.** At a heavy-set test the expectation gap for \(-1_H\) exceeds \(\eta/2\); the same lower bound is imposed on a signed character test. The entropy drop is at least \(3\eta^2/32\) by the exponential moment bound for \([-1,1]\), and starts from at most \(\log p\). The heavy set has size \(<Kk\) whenever nonempty. Each removal reduces remaining mass in counting measure by a fraction at least \(\eta/2\). Truncating and normalizing a stopped law changes its \(\ell^1\) distance by \(2m(H)\le2\eta\) and preserves the required cap. One may restrict the formal transcript list to outputs satisfying that cap. A fitting transcript starts from a known law even after a removal. Each subsequent heavy set is a function of the prefix, so recording its members costs no independent bits. Enumerating a union of all recorded sets multiplies the description length by at most the allowed number of removals. The resulting log counts \(k^{1/10+o(1)}\) for laws and \(k^{3/20+o(1)}\) for unions suffice. Constants such as \(e^{1000}\) are fixed. The shrinkage inequality holds at 5000 and the slack increases above 5000. An empty target or an immediate removal is allowed.

4. **Fitted witnesses and conditioning.** Given \(F\), if subsampling is needed then \(s=r\) and \(s\eta^2\gg\log p\) uniformly. Existence of a Fourier-approximating \(S\) follows for that \(F\); this is not an assertion about simultaneously successful independent samples. With \(g=1_{A_1}*\check\mu\), counting all representations gives \(Y_0(z)\le s\sum_{v\in R}g(z-v)\). Summing \(Y_0\) over \(A_2\) uses \(1_{A_2}*\mu\) with the opposite orientation. The lower coefficient tends to at least \((1/2-b)/2-\varepsilon/\alpha>0.2489\), above \(0.22\). Off the exceptional set the error costs at most \(0.01rs\), and \(\sum Y\le0.01rs\). The clipped bound costs a further \(0.01rs+2rs|E|/k\), giving \(|E|\ge0.095k\), stronger than the \(k/20\) used. Parseval applied to \(1_U*(\check{1_S}-s\check\mu)\) gives exactly the stated mean-square upper bound. Dividing by the threshold squared and using \(|U|/p\le1\), \(r\ge\alpha k/2\) gives the \(1400\) factor. For each fixed transcript, \(R,S\) and exposed \(A_1\), the set of high-error positions is deterministic. At any subsequent draw the denominator is at least \(p-k\), even conditional on a string of prior hits. This justifies the power bound at designated indices. No presumed independence between hit indicators is needed.

5. **The choice count.** Enumerate \(R,S\) even if they depend on the whole cover, retaining only deterministic pairs satisfying the Fourier condition for the specified measure. With \(|V|\ge0.9k\) the relevant containers have \(|P|\ge2r\). The costs of ordering or specifying \(F,T,V\) are unnecessary because the necessary witness uses only \(P,R,S,\mu\). The original log bound divided by \(k\) is below \(-122\) at \(x=5000\), with slope \(2\cdot10^{-5}-1/200<0\). Floors can only reduce the index probability exponent after replacing \(k/20\) by its ceiling, and the given counting bound absorbs the floor of \(r\). The \(O(\log\log p)\) levels cost \(o(k)\) in log count. One can union over all lists first and then perform the adaptive induction on their common event.

6. **Terminal extraction and thinning.** The losses sum to less than \(4k/(3\cdot5000^2)\), including the last active level. For a uniform \(s\)-subset of the retained \(V\), the difference mean is \(r_V(v)(s)_2/(|V|)_2\le s(s-1)/(|V|-1)\). Removing or inserting one point affects at most two directed difference edges; replacement changes the total by at most two in absolute value. The tail exponent \(s^3/k^2\) dominates \(\log p\) even at fixed \(\sigma=10^{-13}\). Thus such an \(S\) exists inside each suitable \(V\) without union over \(V\). Its loss is at most \(bks\), and \(b<d\sigma\) strictly. In particular all these assertions are compatible with \(s^2\ge k\) eventually.

7. **Uniform graph estimate.** Work with the simple undirected difference graph for a fixed \(\ell\)-set, identifying nonzero differences up to sign. Its degree and edge count are at most \(\ell^2\) and \(p\ell^2/2\), even for repeated differences in the defining set. A specified matching has \(2m\) distinct endpoints; summing over edge sets gives \((e/10)^{20\ell^2}\). For larger components, take them in any order until reaching \(20\ell\) vertices. If the last component crosses that threshold, take a connected prefix of length \(\max(3,\text{remaining deficit})\). This gives a total in \([20\ell,20\ell+2]\), even for paths, stars, cycles, cliques or deficits one and two. A connected ordering is supplied by a search tree. A tree traversal of exactly \(2(n-1)\) steps encodes an \(n\)-vertex component subset, so the composition fixes all walk lengths. Sum over encodings of disjoint distinct-vertex sets, charging \(q^v\) only there. The resulting \(p^{(-1/6+o(1))v}\) probability beats \(p^\ell\) at \(v\ge20\ell\); the matching tail also beats this factor since \(\ell=\lceil\log^2 p\rceil\). When there are fewer large-component vertices than the cutoff, all their edges number \(<200\ell^2\). Isolated vertices are harmless and two-vertex components form a matching. The edge bound is monotone, permitting transfer from \(q=2k/p\) to \(A_1\) by an ordered permutation and an independent binomial size. This argument never imposes a degree bound on the induced sample in addition to the ambient bound.

8. **Uniformity for large offset sets.** Fix \(B\) after the graph event. Pin a prescribed unordered pair to the first two sample slots using symmetry. Two distinct unordered pair differences colliding up to sign either force equality of two distinct labels, or yield a relation on three or four slots. At least one unpinned slot has nonzero coefficient in \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\) and is forced after all others are exposed; sufficiently large odd characteristic is essential here. There are at most \(2\ell^4\) signed tests and the denominator is at least \(|B|-\ell\). On the distinct-difference event each undirected edge is counted once, since \(d\ne-d\) for nonzero \(d\). Weight each pair's inclusion probability by the conditional distinctness probability \(\ge1/2\); the graph bound gives a large-set coefficient at most \(440\ell/(\ell-1)<1000\) eventually. This conditional-on-a-pair step handles even a \(B\) chosen using the entire first half.

9. **From offset sets to hit probabilities.** After removing \(r_S(0)=s\), the normalized weights have cap \(2s/(k(s-1))\le1/\lfloor k/4\rfloor\). The capped simplex's extreme points are uniform on that many sites. For any two such supports the ordered off-diagonal sum is bounded by twice the unordered sum on the union, at most \(8000\lfloor k/4\rfloor^2\). The diagonal contributes \(\le2s^4\), the removed atom has squared norm \(\le s^4\), and squaring their sum costs a factor of two. This proves the claimed \(10^5s^4\) bound on one event involving \(A_1\) alone. In the small-loss case \(|A_1+S|\ge ks/3\); hence \(\mathbb EY\ge s^2/(4k)\) eventually. Cauchy–Schwarz gives a fraction at least \(p/(64\cdot10^5k^2)\ge1.5625\cdot10^{-7}>h\) above threshold. At most \(k\) points disappear during exposure, leaving conditional hit probability at least \(h/2\). The binomial comparison has mean at least \(hk/4\), so a half-mean lower tail costs \(\exp(-hk/32)\). The resulting overlap is at least \(hs^2/64>ds^2\). Finally \(\sigma(5001+\log(1/\sigma))<5.04\cdot10^{-10}<h/32\). Only the \(A_1\) graph exception is paid globally. The seed bound is conditional on each admissible \(A_1\) and summed over its deterministic list of possible seeds.

## Companion claims and scope

The average-loss thinning inequality in report.ko.md follows by assigning each represented output to a single center before sampling. The \(s\asymp k/\log k\) entropy rate is \(10^{-9}k+o(k)\), below \(hk/32\). In its separate 1.01 spectral test, the witness has loss at most \(p/640\) with probability at least \(1/5\) and satisfies the Fourier cap with probability \(1-o(1)\). The typical overlap lower bound \(39k/5120-o(k)\) exceeds \(k/180\), the necessary number of exceptional draws exceeds \(k/5\) eventually, and \(2/15>1/8\) pays for the seed choice. These calculations supply a necessary condition on the centers.

In hubs.ko.md the no-edge probability bound for \(m\) signed-difference classes uses \(pm\) undirected edges. Conditioning on absence of nonadjacent previous edges preserves the current edge's probability \(q^2\). Its joint occurrence with an adjacent edge has conditional probability at most \(q^3\) by Harris's inequality for an increasing and a decreasing event. Subtract at most \(4m\) such terms before conditioning on the remaining previous absences, whose probability is at most one. Thus the product upper bound has the stated sign. \(H(0.01)=0.0561\ldots<0.99/9\), and the second independent layer still tests \(\Omega(\ell^2)\) difference classes. Duplicate edge witnesses lie wholly in the two-layer intersection, so deleting its \(O(\log^2p)\) pair differences globally suffices. Uniform extension or shrinkage yields a uniform \(k\)-set by permutation symmetry, with shrinkage an \(o(1)\) exception. A graph of maximum degree two and at least \(3n\) edges contains an \(n\)-matching by greedy deletion. Finally, after excluding center pairs meeting twice inside the exceptional target set, each remaining double-contact pair contributes a collision outside it. The inequality \(\binom m2\le M(m-1)/2\) for \(1\le m\le M\) gives the claimed conditional loss. At fixed \(t\), counting the second center through its ordered label difference gives the rectangle budget; two equal differences with different witnesses may overcount, which is in the required direction. The resulting constant from \(1000(4D/t)^2\ge\gamma(t/2-1)\) exceeds \(10^{-5}\) in the \(t^{3/2}\) bound, and \(b_0^2=o(t)\) absorbs the small-set extension. The fixed-gap proof itself does not need the hub companion.

The statement that some \(C>1\) “suffices” in the opening of fixed-gap.md means suffices for **coverability tending to one**. Specifying that direction would prevent confusion with its proved lower bound. The upstream 1.01 question uses \(\lfloor1.01k\rfloor\), bounded above by \(1.01p/k\); the direction of the implication in the extension is therefore as stated.

## Reproducible checks

Run [check_review.py](check_review.py) with Python 3. The script is independent of the source manuscript's verification script. It checks the old and new strict numerical margins, 896 exact finite-population score means, 533,120 two-sample Lipschitz bounds, 1,533 globally extended functions on a slice, all 32,768 simple graphs on six vertices (188,544 component cutoff cases), and 36,300 distinct-difference edge identities. These finite checks passed. The asymptotic probability bounds and their quantifiers are justified by the proofs above.
